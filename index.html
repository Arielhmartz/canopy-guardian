Birds sing at dawn, at night and all year round. Field campaigns don't.

Most environmental decisions about wildlife still rest on a few days of observation per season. We want every project to be judged on continuous, verifiable evidence.

The problem

Wildlife baselines are short, costly and hard to audit.

Bird baselines and monitoring plans for projects entering Chile's Environmental Impact Assessment System (SEIA) depend on seasonal field campaigns with direct observation — transects and point counts. They are expensive, short, observer-dependent and cover very little of the time and space where birds are actually active.

Gaps in the evidence become delays and extra cost

When baselines fall short, regulators issue observations, request additional campaigns and projects stall. In wind energy, for example, dawn/dusk transects have been questioned as insufficient to assess collision risk — while hundreds of bird collisions have been reported at operating wind farms in Coquimbo.

Snapshots, not series

A few days per season miss nocturnal, cryptic and seasonal species, and say little about trends.

Observer-dependent

Results vary with each specialist's skill and conditions on the day, and can't be re-checked later.

Expensive to repeat

Every extra campaign means specialists, travel and logistics in remote sites — paid by the project owner through the consultancy.

Rising regulatory bar

Law 21.600 (SBAP) and the new biodiversity offset regulation require demonstrating real ecological gains over time — which demands continuous monitoring.

Our solution

One node, three layers of evidence.

Soundscape Sentinel replaces snapshots with continuous acoustic records. Each node processes audio on the device, keeps the raw recordings as backup evidence and sends compact results to a web dashboard with exports formatted for SEIA / RCA files.

Core

Bird species detection

Automatic species occurrence and richness, 24/7, with a model tuned for the avifauna of Chile and the Southern Cone.

Soundscape indices

Acoustic indices (NDSI, ACI, ADI) that track the ecological condition of the site and its change over time.

Environmental variables

Temperature, humidity and anthropogenic noise logged alongside every detection, to explain what drives the patterns.

Alerts & reports

Alerts for threatened species or noise thresholds, a dashboard and exports ready for environmental files.

Auditable by design. Every detection is linked to its audio clip, timestamp and location, so a reviewer or regulator can verify it — something a field notebook can't offer.
How it works

From the dawn chorus to a compliance report.

  1. Listen

    Solar-powered nodes record the soundscape continuously and sense temperature and humidity.

  2. Process on the edge

    On-device AI identifies bird species and computes acoustic indices — no need to stream raw audio.

  3. Transmit

    Compact results travel over 4G or LoRa from remote sites; audio is stored as backup evidence.

  4. Analyse

    The dashboard shows species lists, activity by hour and season, index trends and alerts.

  5. Report

    Exports formatted for baselines, monitoring plans and offset follow-up, ready for the consultant to sign off.

Competition

Recorders and models exist. A compliance service doesn't.

Our main substitute is the field campaign itself. Existing acoustic tools solve parts of the problem, but none delivers continuous, real-time results packaged for environmental assessment in Latin America.
